Our Planet: Balance vs Everyday Pressure

Environmental consciousness isn't about global despair—it's about understanding how everyday micro-actions aggregate into planetary outcomes.

Equilibrium

The Self-Sustaining Biosphere

Natural ecosystems maintain perfect equilibrium: trees absorb ambient carbon, water cycles naturally cleanse rivers, and zero synthetic waste exists. When resources are consumed within regenerative limits, natural habitats flourish with rich biodiversity.

Goal: Keep our resource consumption within circular loops.
Human Footprint

Everyday Industrial Pressures

Rapid urbanization, fossil energy grids, and single-use consumer habits exert relentless strain on natural biomes. In India, over 70% of electricity is coal-fired (0.71 kg CO2e/kWh), and millions of single-use plastic bottles enter landfills daily.

Reality: Everyday choices by 40M+ Indian students have massive leverage.

Mathematical Model & Transparency

How EARTHLY Calculates Impact

EARTHLY separates educational habit analysis from physical activity emissions to maintain scientific integrity.

1. EARTHLY Habit Index (0 – 100)

Lower score = better habits. Normalized points earned within each category are weighted as follows:

Category Score = (Points Earned / Maximum Points) × 100

Overall Score = (Transportation × 0.30)
              + (Food & Dining × 0.30)
              + (Electricity × 0.20)
              + (Shopping & Waste × 0.20)

2. Documented Activity CO₂e Factors

CO₂e is calculated strictly where reliable Indian empirical activity factors exist:

  • Indian Grid Factor: 0.710 kg CO₂e / kWh — Source: Central Electricity Authority (CEA) Baseline Database for Indian Power Sector (User Guide v19).
  • Solo Petrol Car/Cab: 0.140 kg CO₂e / km — Source: Automotive Research Association of India (ARAI).
  • Petrol Two-Wheeler: 0.043 kg CO₂e / km — Source: ARAI urban 110-150cc scooter benchmarks.
  • Bus / Metro Rail: 0.022 kg CO₂e / passenger-km — Source: MoHUA / Urban mass transit studies.
  • Walking / Bicycle: 0.000 kg CO₂e / km — Clean zero direct emission.

3. Scope Boundaries

Food supply chain and fashion production life-cycle emissions are excluded from the quantitative kilogram figure due to vast localized variances. They are addressed through the transparent Habit Index.
